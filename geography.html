Mountains, Deserts and Seas

Geography of Iran

Mountains

Iran has many impressive mountains and is home to two major mountain ranges: the Alborz and the Zagros. The Alborz Mountains stretch across northern Iran, near the Caspian Sea, while the Zagros Mountains extend through western and southwestern parts of the country. Mount Damavand, located in the Alborz range, is Iran's highest peak at about 5,610 metres. These mountains influence Iran's climate, provide water for rivers, and attract visitors who enjoy hiking, climbing, and nature.

Deserts

Iran has large desert regions, including the Dasht-e Kavir and the Dasht-e Lut, which are mainly located in the central and southeastern parts of the country. These deserts are known for their dry climate, very low rainfall, and extreme temperatures. The Dasht-e Lut features remarkable sand dunes and unusual rock formations shaped by wind and erosion. Despite the harsh conditions, some plants and animals have adapted to survive in these environments. Iran's deserts are also famous for their unique landscapes, clear night skies, and natural beauty.

Coastlines

Iran has coastlines along both the north and south of the country. In the north, the Caspian Sea borders Iran and is surrounded by green landscapes, forests, and popular seaside towns. In the south, Iran has coastlines along the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman. These southern coastal areas are important for fishing, trade, shipping, and tourism. Islands such as Kish and Qeshm attract visitors with their beaches, marine life, and natural attractions. Iran's coastlines are important parts of its economy, environment, and cultural life.

Forests and Countryside

Iran has a wide variety of natural landscapes, including green forests, fertile valleys, plains, and rural areas. The northern regions, especially near the Caspian Sea, are famous for their dense forests and high rainfall. The Hyrcanian forests, which stretch along parts of the southern Caspian coast, are among the world's ancient forest ecosystems. In other parts of Iran, villages are surrounded by mountains, farms, gardens, and rivers. These landscapes support local communities, agriculture, and wildlife, while offering beautiful places for visitors to explore.
